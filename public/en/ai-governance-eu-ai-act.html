AI governance and the EU AI Act

Your teams already use AI, and your European clients are starting to ask about the AI Act. We help you know which AI you use, control its risks and prove it.

What AI governance covers

Know which AI you use

An inventory of AI tools and systems, built in-house or bought, with their use, data and owner.

Classify the risks

Which uses are harmless, and which affect people, decisions or sensitive data.

Clear rules

An AI usage policy for your teams: what is allowed, what is not, and who approves what.

Evidence

Technical documentation, evaluations, logs and human approval: what you need to answer a client, an auditor or an authority.

Our approach

  1. Assessment

  2. Gap analysis

Frequently asked questions

Does the EU AI Act apply to a Tunisian company?

It can. Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 also applies to providers established outside the Union that place an AI system on the EU market, or whose output is used in the Union. A Tunisian company selling AI software or services to European clients should therefore pay attention.

What is the timeline?

It is phased: prohibited practices since February 2025, general-purpose AI models since August 2025, then most other obligations from August 2026 and 2027. Postponements have been proposed for part of the high-risk systems: we check the text in force at the time of each engagement.

And in Tunisia?

Organic Law No. 2004-63 on personal data protection applies whenever an AI system processes personal data, under the oversight of the INPDP. Good AI governance helps meet both frameworks at once.

Are you a law firm?

No. We cover the technical and organisational side: inventory, risk assessment, documentation, testing, controls. For legal interpretation, we work with your legal counsel.
