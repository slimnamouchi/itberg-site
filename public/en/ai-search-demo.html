      What happens when you type

      No magic, and no large language model: four simple building blocks, fast and measurable, the same ones we use in our EasyFind app (case study in French).

      1. Normalise

      Lower case, accents and short vowels removed, variants of alif and tāʼ marbūṭa unified, repeated letters shortened (“7leeeeb”).

      2. A pivot form

      Arabic and Arabizi are reduced to the same consonants: “7lib”, “hlib” and “حليب” (milk) all become hlb. Digits get their letter back: 3 for ع, 7 for ح, 9 for ق, 5 for خ.

      3. A lexicon in three scripts

      A small dictionary links derja, Arabic, French and English: “blombi”, “سبّاك”, “plombier”, “plumber”. That is what makes the difference.

      4. Tolerate typos

      An edit distance accepts one letter too many, too few or swapped, more so as the word gets longer.

      What about AI models? They help with long questions, but they cost more and are less predictable. We add them last, preferably running on the device, and only when measurement on a test set shows a gain.

      Going further: generative AI, on your device

      Ask a question about ITBerg. The assistant finds the passages of the site that answer it; if you wish, a generative AI running in your browser then writes an answer from them, citing them. The model, about 1 GB, is only downloaded with your consent, once. Your question never leaves your device.
