AI · Tunisian Arabic

A chatbot that understands Tunisian Arabic

Your customers write in Derja, Arabic and French, often all three at once, and in Latin letters. We build chatbots and assistants that understand them, answer from your information, and hand over to your team when needed.

The challenge: people write the way they speak

Three scripts for one request

“What is the price”, “قداش السوم”, “9adech essoum”: the chatbot must see the same question.

No official spelling

Derja is written in countless ways, with or without digits, mixed with French. Variants and typos must be tolerated.

Answers that commit you

Prices, opening hours, terms: a mistake is costly. The chatbot answers from your information, not its imagination.

A human on hand

Complaints, sensitive or misunderstood cases: hand-over to your team is designed in from the start.

How we build it

  1. Collect real messages

    With your consent and without unnecessary personal data: the questions your customers actually ask, as they write them.

  2. Normalise and link

    Arabizi mapped to the Arabic alphabet, variants grouped, Derja, Arabic and French vocabulary linked.

  3. Answer from your sources

    Search in your documents and product sheets (RAG), cited answers, and “I don’t know” when nothing is found.

  4. Measure before launch

    A reference set of messages, part of it frozen, checks understanding before every update.

Frequently asked questions

Can a chatbot understand Tunisian Arabic (Derja)?

Yes, if it is prepared for it. Tunisians write in Arabic, in French, and often in Derja typed in Latin letters with digits (“3aslema”, “9adech”), with no fixed spelling. We normalise these scripts and measure understanding on real messages before going live.

Do large models like ChatGPT understand Tunisian?

Unevenly: well on some messages, poorly on others, especially in Arabizi. That is why we do not choose a model on reputation but on a measurement with your own conversations, and we add a layer of normalisation and Tunisian vocabulary.

Does the chatbot reply in Derja?

It can reply in French, Modern Standard Arabic or a register close to Derja, depending on your preference and on what the measurement shows to be most reliable. For binding answers (prices, terms), pre-approved text is often safer.

What happens when it does not understand?

It says so, asks a clarifying question, or hands over to someone on your team with a summary of the conversation. A chatbot that makes up an answer costs more than one that admits it does not know.
